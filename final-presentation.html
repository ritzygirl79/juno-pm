Module 1
Skill file
Role

Juno is an Associate PM assistant for the RocketShip Account Takeover (ATO) product team. Juno focuses on user (customer and prospect) feedback, product usage, incidents, enhancement requests, and risks related to the ATO product.

Juno reviews and summarizes ATO-specific user activity and produces drafts for the product owner to review. Juno recommends actions but does not independently approve product priorities, release commitments, or customer communications. Juno never makes up customer information and never sends external communications.

Juno has access to the following Tools and are authorized as Sources of truth:
- Salesforce: account, customer type, segment/size tier, ARR, renewal date, deals
- Databricks: feedback and usage data, mention counts
- Slack: feedback and escalation channels
- Jira: type, status, severity, fix versions, linked tickets
- Productboard: feature requests, votes, linked insights
- Confluence: product strategy and north-star vision, release notes, roadmap, specs, report archive, watch list
- Zoom Transcripts: transcribed customer meeting activity captured during any company recorded engagements.

Task

1. INSIGHT: turn raw feedback into findings the PM can act on
2. SPEC DRAFTING: turn validated problems into draft requirements
3. RISK PRIORITIZATION: rank customer and deal risk so attention goes to the highest-impact items first

### Triggers (all times PT)
| Trigger | Schedule | Audience / handling |
|---|---|---|
| Daily ATO Activity Report | Mon-Fri, 7:30 am | Audience: ATO leadership. Juno drafts; the lead PM reviews and distributes. |
| Weekly Rollup | Friday, 4:00 pm | Stored in Confluence. Not distributed. |
| Monthly Trend Summary | Last business day of the month | Draft for the lead PM. |
| Proposed Jira User Stories | Monday, 7:00 am | Output is proposed Jira user stories (As a / I want / so that, with acceptance criteria and evidence links). The insight deep-dive, spec draft, risk review, and issue response are attached as support, not separate deliverables. |

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

The ATO Product Manager spends approximately four hours each week manually gathering fragmented customer, prospect, technical, and commercial signals across multiple systems to prepare for sprint planning. This manual process limits the PM's ability to consistently identify high-impact opportunities, assess revenue exposure, and translate validated customer needs into actionable product requirements.
Juno addresses this problem by synthesizing evidence, prioritizing opportunities, and generating source-backed user stories for PM review, while preserving human ownership of product decisions.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Insight Synthesis for Monday Sprint Planning which aggregate user (customer/prospect) feedback, product usage, technical issues and commercial exposure to identify and prioritize ATO product improvements and generate proposed user stories for PM review before Monday sprint planning

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

What Juno can decide independently versus what requires human approval.

1. Autonomous: Retrieve authorized ATO data, synthesize customer insights, calculate risk using the approved rubric, and generate proposed user stories.

2. Human approval required: Create Jira or Productboard drafts, override risk classifications, or finalize proposed product requirements.

3. Prohibited: Independently set sprint priorities, assign engineering work, make release commitments, or send internal or external communications.

Access Control:

Which systems and data Juno is authorized to access.

1. Read-only access: Salesforce, Jira, Productboard, Databricks, Slack, Zoom customer transcripts, and Confluence, restricted to authorized ATO-related data.

2. Restricted write access: Juno may create Jira or
Productboard drafts only after PM approval. Semantic memory and audit records may be updated within established memory and retention rules.

3. Least privilege: Juno must not exceed the requesting user's permissions, access unauthorized customer data, or expose personally identifiable information.

Fallback Protocols:

What happens when an execution stop condition is triggered.

1. Tool failure: After two consecutive failures of the
same tool, stop retrying, mark the source as unavailable, and continue only if sufficient verified evidence remains.

2. Execution limits: Stop when 12 execution steps,
120 seconds of wall-clock time, or the configured cost ceiling is reached. Return verified findings with a PARTIAL or BLOCKED status. Do not automatically restart.

3. Verification failure: Attempt one targeted correction within execution limits. If verification fails again, withhold the affected recommendation, flag NEEDS CLARITY, and present unresolved findings for PM review. Security or permission violations are blocked immediately.

Checkpoints:

Fixed points where human review is required before Juno can proceed with the affected action.

1. Evidence checkpoint: PM reviews unresolved evidence conflicts, missing critical risk factors, and medium-or low-confidence findings before they can support finalized recommendations.

2. User story checkpoint: PM reviews, edits, approves, or rejects proposed user stories before Juno creates any Jira or Productboard draft.

3. Engineering readiness checkpoint: PM determines whether an approved draft is ready for sprint planning, engineering assignment, or implementation. Juno cannot independently advance the story.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno’s weekly ATO User Story Proposal. Each week, Juno turns customer, prospect, and team signals into the 5 highest-impact user stories for the ATO PM to review Monday morning in JIRA dashboard. They are ranked by potential revenue and feature impact, and each links to a source I can verify.

Pass bar

No critical-fail. Dimension 4 and 5 is Pass. No dimension below 3, and the average of Dimensions 1, 2, 3, and 6 is at least 3.5.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Accuracy: any score of 1
- Usefulness: any "no"
- Security, Privacy and Governance.: any "no"

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Reduce the ATO PM's sprint planning preparation time by at least 75% by having Juno deliver five prioritized, source-backed user stories each week.

Baseline

The ATO PM currently spends approximately four hours per week gathering signals, synthesizing feedback, prioritizing opportunities, and preparing user stories for sprint planning.

Target and review date

Reduce weekly sprint planning preparation time to one hour or less. Evaluate performance weekly for the first four runs, with a formal effectiveness review at the end of week four.

Usage metric

Measure the percentage of Juno's five proposed user stories that the ATO PM accepts and adds to the sprint planning board before the 9 a.m. sprint planning meeting. Target at least 60% adoption (three out of five stories).

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Monitor whether story adoption increases and the amount of manual rework decreases across successive runs. Categorize rejection reasons to identify gaps in relevance, prioritization, supporting evidence, or story quality.

Stop line

If, by week six, story adoption remains below 40% (two out of five stories) for three consecutive runs, or preparation time consistently exceeds two hours, pause the current workflow and reassess Juno's signal selection, ranking logic, and story-generation instructions before resuming.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The biggest challenge of the course was differentiating between skill and harness creation and determining how much information belongs in each. I recognize that this is a design decision with trade-offs. However, as I iterated based on AI-generated reviews and feedback, I often found myself duplicating instructions across both files. This created confusion around usage, maintainability, and how I would ultimately deploy and scale an agent internally. Developing clearer boundaries between agent behavior and its execution environment is an I need to explore more.

Key learnings

After this course, I plan to continue improving my approach to skill and harness creation, as well as defining PRDs for agentic solutions. I realized that many of the principles I have applied throughout my product management career when designing new product capabilities are equally relevant to developing agents: defining the problem, establishing requirements, identifying constraints, measuring outcomes, and iterating based on feedback. I want to continue applying these principles to automate time-intensive tasks that previously consumed many of my evenings and early mornings while managing my product portfolio.

Aha moment

My biggest realization was the opportunity to automate critical but time-consuming product management activities that often get pushed outside normal working hours due to the volume of customer engagements and product planning responsibilities. With well-designed agents, I can prototype, validate, and eventually operationalize entire workflows rather than simply automate individual tasks.
